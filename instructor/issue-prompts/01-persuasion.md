# Issue 1 — Persuasion and Human Decision-Making

## Objective

Create the first chapter of the guide.

## Required Output

`book/01-persuasion.md`

## Acceptance Criteria

The chapter must include:

- a plain-language definition of persuasion
- an explanation of why persuasion is not the same thing as manipulation
- a concise introduction to Robert Cialdini's major principles of influence
- at least two other useful ideas from psychology, behavioral economics, rhetoric, or communication
- a table showing principle → mechanism → ethical use → misuse risk
- one Mermaid diagram showing a simple decision journey
- a short section titled `Questions to Ask Before Trying to Persuade`
- a short white T-shirt example

## Prompt to Give Your AI Assistant

You are creating Chapter 1 of a short first-year college guide about persuasion, branding, and design.

Create `book/01-persuasion.md`.

Write in a conversational, intelligent tone. Assume the reader is new to the subject but capable of thinking seriously.

Explain persuasion as a tool for helping shape attention, interpretation, trust, and action. Distinguish persuasion from coercion and manipulation.

Introduce Robert Cialdini's major principles of influence accurately and concisely. Then broaden the chapter with at least two additional useful concepts from psychology, behavioral economics, rhetoric, or communication.

Include:
- concrete examples
- a comparison table
- a valid Mermaid diagram
- ethical cautions
- questions a designer should ask before trying to persuade someone

Use a recurring example: a plain white T-shirt. Show how the same physical product can be framed differently without changing the shirt itself.

Do not invent sources, quotations, or URLs. If outside research would improve the chapter, add a section called `Explore Further` containing search suggestions rather than fabricated links.

Finish with a concise summary called `What You Should Remember and Take`.
