# One Shirt, Many Meanings

## A Short Guide to Persuasion, Branding, and Design

How can the same plain white T-shirt suggest adventure, confidence, rebellion, or belonging? This first-year college guide explores how persuasion, brand archetypes, and visual language shape an offer's meaning. The final chapter connects those ideas to directing and reviewing AI-assisted work.

The recurring T-shirt campaigns are hypothetical teaching examples. Historical references and theoretical frameworks are identified in the chapters; a convincing presentation never substitutes for evidence about a product.

## Read the Chapters in Order

1. [Persuasion and Human Decision-Making](01-persuasion.md) — Attention, trust, influence principles, and the boundary between persuasion and manipulation.
2. [Brand Archetypes and Meaning](02-archetypes.md) — Recognizable narrative patterns, audience identity, and the importance of cultural context.
3. [Visual Design Carries Ideas](03-design-language.md) — Modernism, Bauhaus, Swiss typography, postmodern responses, and reading design critically.
4. [One White T-Shirt, Four Different Invitations](04-white-tshirt-case-study.md) — Four campaign concepts combining audience, meaning, persuasion, and visual choices.
5. [Directing the Work, Checking the Result](05-synthesis.md) — Specifications, AI-assisted work, automated checks, human judgment, and version control.

## How to Use This Guide

Read Chapters 1–3 as complementary lenses, then use Chapter 4 to compare them in action. Keep asking what changed in the presentation and what stayed true about the shirt. Try the short exercises with a classmate and compare interpretations rather than searching for one correct archetype.

Use Chapter 3's museum-research instructions to find authentic collection records. Verify sources and distinguish documented facts from your own visual interpretation. Preview the Mermaid diagrams in a Markdown viewer that supports Mermaid.

Finish with Chapter 5 and its Questions for Next Week. Review the guide as an editor: revise an example, challenge an unsupported assumption, and be ready to explain what you changed and learned. For each practical issue, keep its work on a separate branch and inspect the result before accepting it.

This guide was built through the five practical issues. Future additions should likewise begin with a bounded task and a reviewable output. You remain responsible for the meaning and accuracy of the work you submit.
